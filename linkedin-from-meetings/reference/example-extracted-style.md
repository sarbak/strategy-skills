## How he builds a narrative

Five moves, in this order. Most of his good posts use four or five of them.

1. **Open on the thesis.** *(Stated preference, 2026-08-26. It overrides the
   historical pattern below, which was to open on the situation.)* One sentence
   carrying the claim the post exists to make, delivered flat, with no hook
   engineering. It has to land for someone who has just scrolled past something
   unrelated, so it names the domain, the actor and the stake on its own. Under
   about 140 characters. His own best-performing post already did this without
   meaning to: *"We are trying to find a good AEO/GEO/AI visibility tool to
   incorporate into our Tin Computer platform but I am very disappointed with the
   tools in the market"* puts the disappointment in sentence one.

   The old pattern, kept because it explains the rest of his posts: he opened on a
   flat declarative about what he and his team were doing right now. *"We are trying to find a good AEO/GEO/AI visibility tool to incorporate
   into our Tin Computer platform but I am very disappointed with the tools in the
   market."* *"We're open-sourcing our internal markdown editing + collaboration
   tool."* *"We had a great office-warming party + demo yesterday at our new
   office."*
2. **Name the friction.** What is annoying, missing, or harder than it should be.
   *"It is not as fun to iterate them over time."* *"We couldn't find anything that
   made it easy to share and work on the same file."*
3. **Report what everyone else says, then disagree with it.** He quotes the
   consensus before he takes it apart. *"When I ask my peers, I hear 'AEO/GEO work
   is basically same as SEO work'. Clearly, these AI models work very differently
   than Google's search engine and should have very different mechanisms for
   influence."*
4. **Widen once, briefly.** One sentence that places the small thing inside a
   large one, usually built on a parallel. *"In the next decade, millions of people
   will be launching AI-enabled side businesses, the way millions of people
   launched internet-enabled side businesses."* He does this once per post, never
   twice.
5. **Close on a real question, or on an admission that he does not know.** *"Has
   anyone cracked this stuff?"* *"We'll see where this journey takes me."* This is
   where his comments come from.

## How he builds sentences

- **Flat and declarative.** Subject, verb, object. He does not front-load
  suspense, does not open on a fragment, does not use a colon as a drumroll.
- **The turn lives inside the sentence, not between sentences.** He uses "but",
  "however", and "instead of X, we did Y" to carry the contrast rather than
  breaking it onto its own line for drama.
- **Second person, constantly.** "your companies", "your products", "if you
  started getting AI to do your work, then you probably are too". He writes to one
  person.
- **Real paragraphs, two to four sentences.** He does not do the one-line-per-
  paragraph LinkedIn staircase. When he needs a list he uses a plain hyphen or
  bullet and keeps the items long and specific.
- **Concrete nouns from his own week.** Tools, file formats, cities, names. Rarely
  abstractions.
- **Casual asides survive into the final text.** "sooo", "asap", "the cutest
  person in the world". He does not sand these off and they are half of why he
  sounds like a person.
- **He concedes easily.** "I don't like how...", "We'll see", "I am very
  disappointed". He never writes from a position of having figured it out.
- **He names people generously**, often in long runs of tags.
- **No emoji. No hooks. No hashtags** except on posts over nine months old.

## The gap in his own writing

Almost none of his posts carry a number. His work produces striking numbers every
week (5% against 55%, competitor mentions doubling inclusion) and none of them has
ever appeared on his profile. Adding evidence to move 3 (the disagreement) is the
one change that would most improve his posts, and it does not require him to
change register at all.

## Rules for drafting in this voice

- **Thesis first, delivered flat.** The claim goes in sentence one and has to be
  legible cold. Then the situation, then the evidence.
- **Contractions on.** Chosen 2026-08-26 from a four-model comparison: he picked
  the register with "don't", "isn't", "we've" over the fully spelled-out one. His
  own posts use them, so this is his voice rather than a loosening of it.
- Put the disagreement in explicitly. Say what people believe, then say why it is
  wrong. Do not imply it.
- One widening sentence, maximum.
- End on a question he genuinely wants answered.
- Keep the paragraphs real. Do not break every sentence onto its own line.
- No em dashes (his stated preference, even though a few old posts have them).
- Keep one casual aside per post.
- Never sound resolved.
