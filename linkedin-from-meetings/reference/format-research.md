# LinkedIn format research

Gathered 2026-08. Confidence is marked per claim, because most of what is written
about "the LinkedIn algorithm" is SEO content written to rank for "the LinkedIn
algorithm", not measurement.

## High confidence: structural, consistent across every source

- **Comments in the first 60-90 minutes decide reach.** Every source agrees the
  early engagement window determines whether a post is pushed further. Practical
  consequence: the closing question is the most valuable line in the post, and the
  author should be free to reply for an hour after posting.
- **Dwell time and completion, not clicks.** LinkedIn ranks on how long people
  hold on a post and whether they finish it. A long post people abandon does worse
  than a short one they finish. This is why "longer is better" advice is now
  wrong.
- **External links in the post body suppress distribution.** Consistent across
  every source; the size of the penalty is disputed (figures cited run around
  50-60%, which is soft). Workaround everyone uses: link in the first comment.
- **Tuesday to Thursday beats Monday and weekends.** Every study agrees. Wednesday
  is the most consistently strong day.
- **Saves are the strongest quality signal.** Content worth keeping outranks
  content worth liking.

## Medium confidence: directional, do not over-fit

- **Optimal length around 800-1,400 characters** (roughly 150-300 words). Long
  enough to trigger "see more", short enough to finish.
- **Truncation point**: about 200-210 characters on desktop, around 140 on mobile.
  Test it rather than trusting it. LinkedIn has changed it before.
- **Time of day**: the studies disagree outright. Sprout says 11am-4pm Tue-Thu;
  Buffer (4.8M posts) says 3-8pm; Hootsuite says 8-9am Tue/Wed. When four large
  studies disagree, the honest read is that time of day matters less than the
  content and the first-hour replies. Pick a window, hold it, read your own
  analytics after six weeks.
- **B2B seniority skew**: senior people reportedly check either side of the
  workday (7-8am, 6-7pm) rather than during it. Plausible, weakly sourced.

## Low confidence: widely repeated, thinly evidenced

Numbers like "15 seconds of dwell earns a 40% reach bonus", "responding to
comments within the hour gives +35% visibility", and "text-only posts average 6-8%
engagement" circulate everywhere and trace back to vendor blogs rather than to
data anyone published. Directionally they point the right way. Do not put them in
a post as facts.

Richard van der Blom's Algorithm Insights report (1.3M+ posts, 80,000+ profiles)
is the most credible practitioner source and is where most secondhand figures
originate. Read the primary report rather than the summaries.

## Platform-wide context

Reach is down across LinkedIn year over year: views roughly halved, engagement
down around 25%, follower growth down sharply. This sets the expectation. A good
post in 2026 does the numbers a mediocre post did in 2024. Judge posts against the
author's own baseline, not against screenshots other people share.

## Read your own feed instead of trusting this file

Step 3.6 of the skill tabulates about twenty organic posts from the author's own
top-sorted feed: author, seat, shape, length, reactions, comments. That read is a
better guide to what one specific audience sees than any generic study here, and
it is worth redoing every few months. Keep the table in the style file.
